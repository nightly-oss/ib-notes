The exponent says how many times the base multiplies by itself.
$$
a^n = a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a... (n \space times)
$$


- $n$ is called the exponent.
- $a$ is called the base.

### Laws of Exponents
1. When you multiply an exponent with another, their exponents ***add** together
	$x^α \cdot x^β = x^{α+β}$
2. When dividing, they *subtract*
	$\frac{α^μ}{α^ν} = α^{μ-ν}$
3. When raising a power to another power, multiply the exponents
	$(α^μ)^ν = α^{μν}$
4. You can *distribute* the exponent to each factor inside the parentheses
	$(αβ)^ν = α^ν β^ν$
5. You can distribute the exponent to both the numerator and the denominator
	$(\frac{α}{β})^ν = \frac{α^ν}{β^ν}$
6. If an exponent is zero, it equals to 1
	$α^0 = 1$
7. Invert the number if it's raised to a negative exponent
	$α^{-ν} = \frac{1}{α^ν}$
8. Exponents can be fractions representing roots and vice versa
