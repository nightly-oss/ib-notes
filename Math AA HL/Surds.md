- **Radical:** Any number written under the square root sign ($\sqrt{}$)
- **Surd:** Any real, [[Terminology|irrational]] number.
###### Examples
> This is a radical, *not* a surd: $\sqrt{4} = 2$
> These are radicals *and* surds: $\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}, \sqrt{11}, ...$
###### Definition
> $\sqrt{a}$ is the non-negative number $b$, such as that $b^2 = a$
###### Rules
1. $a \ge 0$
2. $(\sqrt{a})^2 = a$
3. $\sqrt{a \cdot b} = \sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{b}$ when $a,b \ge 0$
4. $\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}$ when $a \ge 0$ and $b > 0$

---

### Exercise Examples
##### Q: Write $\sqrt{72}$ in *simplest form*
$$
\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 6 \sqrt{2}
$$

##### Q. Rationalize $\frac{5}{1 - \sqrt{3}}$
$$
\frac{5}{1 - \sqrt{3}} = \frac{5(1 + \sqrt{3})}{(1 - \sqrt{3})(1 + \sqrt{3})} = \frac{5(1 \sqrt{3})}{1 - 3}
$$
