You may be asked to round a number in two ways. If you're asked to round it:
- **To $n$ decimals**, there should be $n$ number of digits after the decimal point only.
- But in the IB, we also round another way.

### Significant Form
**In significant form**, the digits after the decimal point should stop $n$ numbers that aren't 0.
For example, if asked "round $0.00021042$ to **3** significants", then it should be $0.000210$.
###### When rounding in significant form:
- If the number starts with 0, remove it.
- Once you reach a **significant** (a number that’s after the removed 0), keep it in
- After you reach your limit ($n$ of significants), stop and round.

###### Step-by-Step
> **Example:** Round $0.005439$ to **3** significants.

1. Remove all zeroes until you reach a significant: $X.XX5439$
2. Since the question asks to round to 3 significants, round it: $544$

---

> [!CAUTION]
> **Careful!** When reaching your limit of significants, you still **round** the number, so $5439$ becomes $544$.

> [!CAUTION]
> If a 0 is **AFTER** a significant, it *is* considered a significant, so do *NOT* remove it.
> $$123014 \rightarrow 1230$$
